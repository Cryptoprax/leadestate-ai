# AtlasOS Marketplace

## Vision

The Marketplace enables customers to discover and install reviewed first-party
and third-party modules, integrations, templates, AI tools, and workflow packs.
The ecosystem expands AtlasOS without granting extensions implicit trust.

## Participants

Publishers have verified identities, legal agreements, support contacts, payout
details where applicable, and security posture. Organization administrators are
installers; platform teams govern review, compatibility, policy, and emergency
controls.

## Listing

A listing includes purpose, publisher, media, documentation, pricing,
permissions, data access, regions, compliance claims, dependencies,
compatibility, support, release history, privacy policy, and uninstall behavior.

## Review process

Submission undergoes automated validation, dependency and vulnerability
scanning, permission review, data-flow review, functional testing,
accessibility assessment, performance checks, and human approval proportional
to risk. Updates can trigger renewed review.

## Installation

Installers receive a clear consent screen listing permissions, data categories,
external transfers, costs, dependencies, and organization scope. Installation
is versioned, auditable, idempotent, and reversible where possible.

## Runtime governance

Extensions use scoped APIs, events, secrets, quotas, and egress policy.
Operational metrics cover availability, errors, latency, usage, and suspicious
behavior. AtlasOS can revoke credentials, disable a version, or suspend a
publisher.

## Commercial model

The Marketplace may support free, subscription, usage-based, and negotiated
offerings. Billing defines revenue share, taxes, trials, refunds, payouts, and
disputes without compromising installation permissions.

## Lifecycle

Publishers communicate support and deprecation windows. Removal from discovery
does not silently uninstall customer data. End-of-life workflows provide notice,
export, replacement guidance, and policy-compliant cleanup.
